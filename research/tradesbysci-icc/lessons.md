# SCI trading course | lesson-by-lesson first-pass observations

**Source:** SCI official numbered lessons; see [index](README.md) for video links. These are original summaries of the retrieved captions, not copies of SCI's transcripts. **Only Day 1 received a completed detailed visual review.** Treat all other lesson observations as provisional pending chart validation.

## Day 1 — Trends
SCI defines downtrends using lower highs/lower lows, uptrends using higher highs/higher lows, and discourages trading consolidation. A structure break can suggest a reversal *or* consolidation; it is not automatically a trade entry. **Unresolved:** precise swing identification on real candles.

## Day 2 — Indication
SCI treats a break of a meaningful swing high or swing low, forming a fresh extreme, as the indication that price may push in the breakout direction. His preferred context is often 1H, 4H when clearer. Do **not** buy/sell the initial indication blindly because of subsequent corrections/liquidity sweeps. **Unresolved:** wick vs close and qualified swing selection.

## Day 3 — Liquidity & corrections
SCI explains that after indication, price commonly corrects, potentially shaking out breakout traders. He frames correction through market participant behavior and keeps reference to the 1H and 4H. He says he relies on price action, not conventional indicators. **Unresolved:** algorithmic correction depth, liquidity criteria and when it has finished.

## Day 4 — Entries / continuation
SCI calls continuation his entry model: use the higher-timeframe (often 1H) indication, follow the correction, inspect 15m or 5m structure for reversal back in the intended direction, and wait for a second directional confirmation. For a bearish example he describes entry under structure, stop above previous reaction, and target the 1H indication low instead of an arbitrary 5m target. **Unresolved:** precisely chosen pivot and trigger candle.

## Day 5 — Understand entries more
The lesson emphasizes buyer/seller control around established swing highs/lows and reaction levels rather than reducing ICC to a basic breakout-retest pattern. Higher-timeframe context outranks small countertrend fluctuations. **Unresolved:** which reaction levels SCI considers meaningful when multiple candidates exist.

## Day 6 — Timeframe correlation
SCI explicitly says to mark up the 1H or higher first, then scale to 15m/5m **during correction and continuation**. For shorts, he describes failing to make higher highs, producing lower highs, breaking internal supports, and alignment with the higher-timeframe sell thesis. **Unresolved:** rule for higher-timeframe conflicts and multi-timeframe timing.

## Day 7 — How to mark up charts
Focus is on disciplined annotation of swing highs/lows and relevant levels, instead of overly cluttered charts. **Unresolved:** exact annotated swing examples require visual inspection.

## Day 8 — Avoid overthinking
SCI analyzes a gold trade using a higher-timeframe swing-high area, correction, continuation, and a target at an opposing buyer level. He warns against forcing small-account returns and against unnecessarily complex frameworks. **Unresolved:** chart-specific entry and TP coordinates.

## Day 9 — Identify reversals
SCI says failure to maintain expected trend swings alerts him to a potential directional change; he describes 1H level context, 15m/5m confirmation, and observing volume/session participation. **Unresolved:** objective threshold for a trend transition vs temporary liquidity sweep.

## Day 10 — How to mark up
SCI describes a 'no trade zone' when swing high and low exist without an indication. He mentions pivot-point high/low tools as optional guides but cautions not to rely on them blindly. **Unresolved:** allowable indicator assistance versus SCI's visual marking preference.

## Day 11 — Market structure entries
SCI says ICC is a simple way of teaching price action and stresses live observation over excessive confidence in old backtests. This is *SCI's viewpoint*, not proof that historical testing lacks value. **Unresolved:** how to convert discretionary judgment into reproducible decisions.

## Day 12 — Decision making and psychology
SCI discusses extra confirmation when 1H/4H structure is ambiguous, and specifically discusses BTC in relation to altcoins such as SOL. He treats indecision/no indication as a reason to hold off. **Unresolved:** whether BTC correlation is a required veto or context-only assessment.

## Day 13 — Positioning and psychology
SCI discusses capital discipline and using structural reactions for protective stops: shorts above lower highs, longs below higher lows. **Unresolved:** exact stop buffer, loss limit, partial profit and position-sizing rules.

## Day 14 — Market structure
SCI expands structural examples across higher timeframes and describes how high/low progression informs entry, continuation, stops and targets. He describes market structure as fundamental. **Unresolved:** complete visual chart audit and definitive interpretation of multi-timeframe examples.
